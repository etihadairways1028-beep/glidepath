Every button tells the truth

Glidepath checks the simulator's own data after each tap. You see Done only when the sim actually changed, and a plain warning when an aircraft ignores a control.

  • Lights: beacon, strobes, landing, taxi, nav, logo, wing
  • Parking brake, gear, flaps and pause
  • Safety checks: no gear up on the ground, no engine shutdown in the air

When it goes wrong, it says so

  • !You snatched the rotation at 7° a second. That's how tail strikes happen; pull steadily, about 2–3.5° a second.
  • !Pitched up too far after lift-off, 24° nose up. Follow the flight director, normally 12–18°, or the speed bleeds away.
  • !Not stable at 1,000 ft: gear still up. Most airlines would call a go-around there.
  • !Hard landing at 720 fpm. Beyond about 600 fpm (10 ft/s) most airliners need a hard-landing inspection.

    Honest status

    What works today, and what doesn't yet.

    Glidepath is early. If something isn't built, or hasn't been tested with a real simulator, this list says so.

    Built and tested

    • Flight phases, from preflight to on blocks, including pauses, bounces and go-arounds.
    • Debrief of takeoff, handling, approach and landing, in plain words.
    • Landing grades from seven factors, aircraft-aware, all thresholds published above.
    • Every timing, scheduled against estimated against actual.
    • Copilot answers, alerts and spoken callouts.
    • Recording and logbook on your PC, resumed after a sim restart, with career totals.
    • The app on iPad, iPhone, Android, Mac and PC, in dark and light mode.
    • Glideslope messages at each stage: cruise reached, top of descent, approach, touchdown.
    • Connecting by code: type the 6-digit code from your PC into the app.
    • Flight replay of any recorded flight, on the map and in 3D, with speed control.
    • Checklists for Airbus and Boeing style flying, the right one opening for your phase. Simplified for sim use, not copied from any manual.
    • Badges earned from your own logbook: butter landings, long haul, night landings, airports visited and more.

    Built, waiting for real-world testing

    • Microsoft Flight Simulator 2020 and 2024. Written against Microsoft's documentation; first real test flights are happening now.
    • The Windows app. Packaged and checked, not yet run on many PCs.
    • SimBrief, weather, VATSIM, IVAO and SayIntentions traffic and ATC, rain radar, METAR weather dots, aircraft photos. Built against each service's published format; VATSIM, IVAO and radar checked against the live feeds.
    • Cockpit tools: Fenix and FSLabs EFBs, the GSX remote and FlyByWire SimBridge, opened from your PC. Built on each add-on's documented address, not yet tried with every add-on.
    • Airport maps with taxiways, gates and stands, and a 3D chase view over real terrain.
    • Lights messages and reminders (beacon, strobes, landing lights at 10,000 ft). Work with aircraft that use the simulator's standard light switches; some add-ons, like Fenix, run their own and may not report them.
    • Charts for your flight's airports, picked automatically: the FAA's official airport diagrams, SIDs, STARs and approaches for US airports (free, through your Bridge), and one tap to ChartFox or Navigraph everywhere else. Navigraph doesn't let other apps show its charts.
    • Chart map: the look of a navigation chart, with online VATSIM and IVAO centre sectors drawn as their real airspace and D·G·T·A badges for each airport's controllers.
    • Remote control: autopilot, lights, gear, flaps, parking brake, pause, engine auto-start and camera views. Standard simulator controls; each button checks the sim really changed. Not yet tried in the sim.
    • Live camera from your sim PC to your iPad over Wi-Fi. Checked between two browsers; not yet with the sim.
    • Messages for top of descent in 10 minutes, 10 and 30 minutes to landing, ATC online at your airports, and a debrief message that opens the debrief. Also as notifications when Glidepath is in the background.
    • Live airport boards from VATSIM and IVAO, an animated rain radar, and pilot tools (clocks, crosswind, descent, approach path, fuel, converter).
    • Sharper satellite map from Esri World Imagery near airports and cities, with roads and airport markings on top.
    • Accounts: sign up, sign in, several accounts per device, flights synced between devices. Built on Cloudflare and tested locally; live once the service is deployed. The monthly email needs an email service on top.

    Not built yet

    • Native iPhone and iPad app with a Lock Screen Live Activity.
    • PMDG 737 and 777 CDU on your iPad, through PMDG's SDK.
    • Watching away from home over mobile data (needs a small free relay server).
    • Replying to ATC from the iPad, and X-Plane.

    Questions

    Good to know.

    Is it really free?

    Yes. There is no paid version and nothing is locked. Glidepath runs on free services, and your flight data stays on your own PC and devices.

    Do I need an App Store app?

    No. Open the app link in Safari on your iPad or iPhone and add it to your Home Screen. It also works in any browser on Android, Mac and PC.

    Which simulators work?

    Microsoft Flight Simulator 2020 and 2024. X-Plane isn't supported yet.

    Will remote control work with PMDG, Fenix or FSLabs?

    Partly. Glidepath uses the simulator's standard controls. Default aircraft respond to all of them; study-level add-ons run their own systems and may ignore some. Every button tells you whether the sim actually changed.

    Can I watch my flight away from home?

    Not yet. Today everything runs over your home Wi-Fi. Watching over mobile data needs a small free relay server and is next on the list.

    Do I need to put anything in my Community folder?

    No. The Bridge talks to the sim through SimConnect, which is built into Microsoft Flight Simulator.

    Is my data private?

    Live telemetry never leaves your network. If you make an account, only flight summaries are stored, so your logbook follows you between devices. Delete your account in the app and it's all gone.

    Windows says it protected my PC. Is that safe?

    Windows shows that for new apps that aren't code-signed yet. Check the file's SHA-256 against the one on this page, then choose More info and Run. The code is open on GitHub.

    Installing, step by step

    1. Run the installer. Windows asks for permission once: the installer lets your iPad reach the Bridge through Windows Firewall.
    2. If Windows shows “Windows protected your PC”: that's SmartScreen. It appears for new apps that aren't code-signed yet (signing is coming). You can check the file first: its SHA-256 should match the one on this page. Then choose More info and Run.
    3. Open Glidepath Bridge from the desktop. It shows a 6-digit code.
    4. On your iPad, open the Glidepath app and type the code.
    Something not working?
    • The iPad can't connect: make sure it's on the same Wi-Fi as the PC. If the code isn't found, tap Type my PC's address instead and enter the numbers from the Bridge window, or open glidepath.local:4810 in Safari.
    • The sim isn't found: start MSFS and load into a flight, then run Test MSFS connection from the Start menu and send the report.
    • Anything else: Start menu › Glidepath › Bridge logs, and send the newest log file.
